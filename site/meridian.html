(02) — The Creed
(01) — Escapement

A free-sprung balance, adjusted in six positions.

No index regulator. Rate is corrected by four gold weights on the rim, moved a fraction of a degree at a time until the deviation falls under two seconds a day.

(02) — Finishing

Anglage cut by hand, at forty-five degrees.

Every bridge edge is chamfered and polished against tin and diamond paste. Eleven hours of work that only reveals itself when the light moves across it.

(03) — Reserve

Seventy-two hours, from one barrel.

A single mainspring, wound slowly, holds three days of tension — set down on Friday, it is still keeping time on Monday morning.

(03) — Position
We do not make watches quickly. We make roughly two hundred a year, and we are told this is a problem.

Every calibre leaves the atelier with the name of the watchmaker who assembled it engraved on the underside of the balance cock. If it ever returns, it returns to them.

Élise Roubaud  —  Head of Atelier

The Atelier

(04) — Rue du Rhône, Geneva
Fig. i — Balance bridge
Nine months, one pair of hands

A watchmaker is given one movement and no deadline.

They are not permitted to work on a second until the first is regulated, cased, and worn for thirty days. It is an inefficient way to run a workshop, and the only way we have found to make something worth keeping.
